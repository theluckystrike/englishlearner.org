How long does it take to learn English?

A1 to C2 About 10 minutes

The key idea

Cambridge English publishes approximate guided learning hours for each CEFR level. They are a guideline, not a promise.

See the hoursPlan your weeks

Most learners want a number. Cambridge English publishes approximate guided learning hours for every CEFR level, from complete beginner to C2. Here are the numbers and what they do and do not mean.

Guided learning hours per level

Approximate guided learning hours from beginner level (cumulative)
CEFR levelCambridge examHours (approximate)
A1A1 Starters/Movers (YLE)90 to 100
A2A2 Key (KET)180 to 200
B1B1 Preliminary (PET)350 to 400
B2B2 First (FCE)500 to 600
C1C1 Advanced (CAE)700 to 800
C2C2 Proficiency (CPE)1,000 to 1,200

These hours are cumulative. They count from complete beginner level, not from one level to the next. Cambridge English says that these figures are intended as a guideline only.

Source: Cambridge English support, "Guided learning hours", https://support.cambridgeenglish.org/hc/en-gb/articles/202838506-Guided-learning-hours. The page showed "Updated 15 October 2026". This lesson read it on 15 October 2026.

From one level to the next

Cambridge English gives a simpler rule for moving up one level. It takes approximately 200 guided learning hours to progress from one CEFR level to the next. For example, a learner who has passed B2 First might need approximately 200 hours of lessons and supervised study to prepare for C1 Advanced.

Both the table and this rule are approximate. Use the table when you count from complete beginner level. Use the rule when you count from one level to the next.

What each level means

The hours tell you how long. The CEFR global scale tells you what you can do at each level. The table below gives the Council of Europe description of each level in simpler words.

The CEFR global scale in simple English
LevelUser groupWhat you can do
A1Basic userYou can understand and use familiar everyday expressions and very basic phrases. You can introduce yourself and answer simple questions about personal details. You can talk in a simple way if the other person speaks slowly and clearly and is ready to help.
A2Basic userYou can understand sentences and common expressions about things that matter to you every day, such as family, shopping, your local area and work. You can do simple, routine tasks that need a short, direct exchange of information. You can describe your background and your surroundings in simple words.
B1Independent userYou can understand the main points of clear standard language about familiar things at work, at school and in free time. You can deal with most situations while you travel where the language is spoken. You can write simple connected text on familiar topics. You can describe experiences, events, hopes and plans, and give short reasons for your opinions.
B2Independent userYou can understand the main ideas of complex text on concrete and abstract topics, including technical discussions in your own field. You can talk with native speakers with some fluency, and the conversation is not hard work for either side. You can write clear, detailed text on many subjects and explain the advantages and disadvantages of different options.
C1Proficient userYou can understand a wide range of long and difficult texts and notice meaning that is not stated directly. You can speak fluently without searching much for words. You can use the language well for social, academic and work purposes. You can write clear, well-organised, detailed text on complex subjects.
C2Proficient userYou can understand almost everything that you hear or read with ease. You can summarise information from different spoken and written sources in a clear way. You can express yourself very fluently and precisely, and show fine differences of meaning, even in complex situations.

Source: Council of Europe, CEFR global scale, Table 1 (CEFR 3.3), https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/table-1-cefr-3.3-common-reference-levels-global-scale. This lesson read it on 15 October 2026 and wrote each description in simpler words.

What changes the time

Cambridge English names some factors that can change how long it takes to improve your English. They include these four.

  • Your language learning background.
  • How intensive your study is.
  • Your age.
  • How much you study or use English outside lesson times.

Cambridge English also says that you may need more or less time and support depending on your own needs. This lesson does not calculate the effect of these factors.

How to find your level

Before you plan hours, find your current level. Take the level test, then read the section From one level to the next. Cambridge English says one level takes approximately 200 guided learning hours. Read about the CEFR levels for more about each level.

The numbers answer "how long", not "how hard". Use them to plan, not as a guarantee.
