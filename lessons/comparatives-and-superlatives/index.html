Comparatives and superlatives

A2 About 10 minutes

The key idea

Short adjectives take -er and -est. Long adjectives take more and the most. Put than after a comparative.

Read the rulesTry the quiz

Use a comparative to compare two things: "Tom is taller than Ben." Use a superlative to compare one thing with all the others in a group: "Tom is the tallest in the class." This lesson gives you the rules in order, from the easiest to the hardest.

How to form comparatives and superlatives

The form depends on the length of the adjective. Count the syllables, the beats in the word: tall has one, happy has two, expensive has three.

How to form comparatives and superlatives
AdjectiveComparativeSuperlativeRule
tall, cheap, fasttaller, cheaper, fasterthe tallest, the cheapest, the fastestOne syllable: add -er and -est.
nice, safe, largenicer, safer, largerthe nicest, the safest, the largestEnds in e: add -r and -st.
big, hot, thinbigger, hotter, thinnerthe biggest, the hottest, the thinnestOne vowel + one consonant: double the consonant.
easy, happy, busyeasier, happier, busierthe easiest, the happiest, the busiestEnds in consonant + y: change y to i.
modern, famous, expensivemore modern, more famous, more expensivethe most modern, the most famous, the most expensiveMost adjectives with two or more syllables: use more and the most.

Use a comparative to compare two things

Put than after the comparative. Than introduces the second thing.

  • My brother is taller than me.
  • This phone is cheaper than that one.
  • The train is faster than the bus.
  • Is history more difficult than maths?

Use the and a superlative for the top of a group

A superlative compares one thing with all the others in a group. Put the before it.

  • Mount Everest is the highest mountain in the world.
  • She is the best student in our class.
  • This is the most expensive hotel in the city.
  • It was the worst day of my life.

Use in with places and groups: in the world, in the class, in my family. Use of with time and numbers: of my life, of the year, of the three.

As ... as and not as ... as

Use as + adjective + as when two things are the same. Use not as ... as when they are different. The adjective does not change.

  • My bag is as heavy as yours. They weigh the same.
  • The bus is not as fast as the train. The train is faster.

Make a comparison stronger or softer

Put much, a lot or far before a comparative to make the difference big. Put a bit, a little or slightly before it to make the difference small. Do not use very with a comparative.

  • This flat is much bigger than our old one.
  • The blue shirt is a bit cheaper than the red one.
  • The second test was less difficult than the first. Less is the opposite of more.

Mistakes Chinese speakers often make

In Chinese, the adjective does not change after 比 or 最. In English, the adjective itself changes. That is why these mistakes are common.

Incorrect

My phone is cheap than yours.

Correct

My phone is cheaper than yours.

Use the comparative form before than.

中文说“我的手机比你的便宜”，形容词不变；英语要把 cheap 变成 cheaper。

Incorrect

He is more tall than his father.

Correct

He is taller than his father.

Tall has one syllable. Add -er. Do not use more.

单音节形容词加 -er，不用 more。

Incorrect

This book is more better.

Correct

This book is better.

Better is already a comparative. Do not add more.

better 已经是比较级，前面不要再加 more。

Incorrect

She is tallest girl in the class.

Correct

She is the tallest girl in the class.

Put the before a superlative.

最高级前面要加 the。“最高的”要说 the tallest。

Incorrect

Today is very hotter than yesterday.

Correct

Today is much hotter than yesterday.

Do not use very with a comparative. Use much or a lot.

比较级前不能用 very，要用 much 或 a lot。“热多了”就是 much hotter。

Superlatives use the article the. For more about the, read articles in English.

Questions learners ask

Is it "taller than me" or "taller than I"?

Both are correct. In speech, most people say She is taller than me. A little more formal: She is taller than I am. "Taller than I" alone sounds very formal.

Do I always need "the" before a superlative?

Usually, yes: the best, the most popular. After my, your, his or her, do not add the: She is my best friend.

Which two-syllable adjectives take -er?

Adjectives that end in -y take -ier: happy, happier. A few others, such as quiet, clever and narrow, can take -er or more. Most other two-syllable adjectives take more: more careful, more famous, more modern.

What is the difference between "farther" and "further"?

For distance, both are correct: The station is further than I thought. Only further can also mean "more" or "extra": Please contact us for further information.
