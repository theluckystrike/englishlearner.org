Passive voice

B1 to B2 About 18 minutes

The key idea

Use be + past participle to put the person or thing receiving an action first. Change be to show the tense.

Read the rulesTry the quiz

The passive voice puts the receiver of an action in subject position. Compare active and passive voice in “A chef cooked the meal” and “The meal was cooked by a chef”. These passive voice rules show how to change active to passive, with examples across tenses.

Passive voice rules across tenses

The usual pattern is subject + be + past participle. The past participle is the third verb form, such as made, written or taken. Regular past participles end in -ed, like repaired. The participle stays the same when the tense changes.

Choose be to match the time and the new subject. Use am with I, is with he, she, it or a singular noun, and are with you, we, they or a plural noun. Use was or were for the past.

Passive forms with repair
Tense or formPatternExample
Present simpleam/is/are + participleThe bike is repaired here every year.
Present continuousam/is/are being + participleThe bike is being repaired now.
Past simplewas/were + participleThe bike was repaired yesterday.
Past continuouswas/were being + participleThe bike was being repaired when I called.
Present perfecthas/have been + participleThe bike has been repaired.
Past perfecthad been + participleThe bike had been repaired before I arrived.
Willwill be + participleThe bike will be repaired tomorrow.
Modalcan/must/should + be + participleThe bike must be repaired.
Going toam/is/are going to be + participleThe bike is going to be repaired.

Keep every helping verb that the meaning needs. Being marks an action in progress. Been follows have in a perfect passive. In a simple modal passive, can, must or should takes be, without an ending.

Positive, negative, question and short answers
Sentence typeExample
PositiveThe doors are locked at nine.
NegativeThe doors are not locked at eight.
QuestionAre the doors locked at nine?
Short answersYes, they are. / No, they aren’t.
Perfect questionHas the parcel been sent?
Perfect short answersYes, it has. / No, it hasn’t.
Modal questionCan the ticket be changed?
Modal short answersYes, it can. / No, it can’t.

Put not after the first helping verb. Move that verb before the subject for a yes/no question. Don’t add do or did to a be-passive question. In short answers, repeat only the first helping verb.

When the person doing the action is unknown

Use the passive when you don’t know who did something. You can also use it when the person is obvious or unimportant. The person or thing doing the action is called the agent.

  • My bag was stolen on the train.
  • The classrooms are cleaned every evening.
  • Your application has been received.

We don’t need “by someone” in the first example. The other sentences concern the cleaning and the application, rather than the workers.

When the action or result matters most

Start with the topic your reader already knows. The passive helps you keep that topic at the beginning of the next sentence.

  • We bought an old flat. The kitchen was replaced last year.
  • The bridge is being checked, so please use another road.
  • All the tickets have been sold.

Compare “We have sold all the tickets”. That active sentence draws attention to what we have done. Both choices are correct. Choose the one that fits your message.

Passive voice in formal and scientific writing

In a method or process, the steps may matter more than the people. Passive sentences help you describe those steps. These are sample sentences, not reports of a real study.

  • The water was heated slowly.
  • Each sample was placed in a clean container.
  • The results were checked by another researcher.

Formal writing can use active sentences too. “We checked the results” clearly names the researchers. Follow the needs of the text. Don’t change every active sentence just to sound formal.

Passive voice examples in news

News reports often begin with the person, place or event affected. A passive can report an event before the writer knows who caused it. These are invented examples for grammar practice.

  • Several roads were closed after the storm.
  • A missing child has been found.
  • The new library will be opened on Monday.

Use the full verb form in a normal sentence. News headlines sometimes leave out be, as in “Road closed after storm”. That short headline pattern isn’t the usual pattern for body text.

When to include the by-phrase

Add by + agent when the identity matters. Leave it out when it adds no useful information. The passive doesn’t require a by-phrase.

  • The menu was designed by my sister.
  • The window was broken by a falling branch.
  • The form must be signed before Friday.

An agent can be a thing, as the branch example shows. Use with for a tool in sentences such as “The bread was cut with a knife”. After by, use object pronouns, such as me, him, her, us and them.

How to change active to passive

First find the object of the active verb. Ask what or who receives the action. Then follow these steps.

  1. Move the object to subject position.
  2. Keep the tense, but use the right form of be for the new subject.
  3. Change the main verb to its past participle.
  4. Add by + the old subject if that information matters.

The active object “the door” becomes the passive subject “The door”. “Painted” becomes “was painted”. The old subject, Mia, appears after by. There is no direct object in this passive sentence.

Worked example with the past simple

The chef cooked the meals. → The meals were cooked by the chef. Move “the meals” first. Keep past time with were, because meals is plural. Cooked is also the past participle.

Worked example with an action in progress

They are repairing the lift. → The lift is being repaired. The present continuous becomes is being + repaired. Use is for the singular lift. Leave out the unknown workers.

Worked example with the present perfect

She has written the report. → The report has been written by her. Keep has and add been. Written is already the past participle. Her is the object form of she. In real writing, keep “by her” only if the reader knows who she is and her identity matters.

Verbs with two objects

Give, send, offer and teach can take a person and a thing as objects. With these verbs, either object can often become the passive subject.

  • They gave me a book. → I was given a book.
  • They gave me a book. → A book was given to me.
  • The school offered her a place. → She was offered a place.

When the person comes first, use a subject pronoun. Me becomes I, and her becomes she. When the thing comes first in these examples, use to before the person. This pattern doesn’t work with every verb. Say “The rule was explained to me”, not “I was explained the rule”.

The get-passive in informal English

In conversation, get + past participle can describe something that happens to a person or thing. It often describes a change or an unexpected event.

  • My phone got damaged in my bag.
  • She got promoted last month.
  • Did anyone get hurt?

The event can be good or bad. For neutral or formal writing, be is usually the safer choice. Get-passive questions and negatives use do in simple tenses, as in “He didn’t get invited”. Don’t treat every get phrase as a passive. “Get tired” can simply mean “become tired”.

Passive forms with reporting verbs

Use it is said that to report what people say without naming them. You can also start with the person or thing being discussed, followed by is said to.

  • It is said that he lives abroad.
  • He is said to live abroad.
  • She is believed to have left yesterday.

The first two sentences report the same idea. Verbs such as think, believe and report also allow these patterns. Use to have + past participle for an action earlier than the reporting. In the last example, the belief is current, but her departure happened yesterday. A reporting passive presents a claim, not proof.

Have something done

Use have + object + past participle when you arrange for another person to do a job for you. Keep the object between have and the participle.

  • I had my hair cut yesterday.
  • We are having the windows cleaned.
  • She will have her bike repaired tomorrow.

“I cut my hair” means I did it myself. “I had my hair cut” usually means someone else cut it. This pattern can also describe an unwanted event, as in “They had their bags stolen”. They didn’t arrange the theft.

Verbs that cannot be passive

Happen, arrive and die are intransitive verbs. They don’t take a direct object, so they cannot form the usual passive.

  • The accident happened at night.
  • Our train arrived late.
  • The plant died in the heat.

Don’t add be before these past forms. “The accident was happened” is incorrect. Some other verbs have both transitive and intransitive uses. Compare “Someone opened the door”, “The door was opened” and “The door opened”. The last sentence is active. The door is its subject, and no agent is named. Some verb + preposition groups allow a passive, as in “The children were looked after by their aunt”. Keep the preposition in this pattern.

Past simple active forms and -ed adjectives

An -ed ending alone doesn’t make a verb passive. Compare “The workers repaired the roof” with “The roof was repaired”. The first sentence is past simple active. The second is past simple passive.

Irregular forms make the difference clearer. Mia wrote the email. The email was written by Mia. Wrote is past simple. Written is the participle used after was.

Some words ending in -ed work as adjectives. I am interested in music. This describes a feeling or state. It isn’t an action performed by music. Compare The students were interviewed by a reporter. That sentence reports an action.

Context can allow both readings. “The door was closed” can describe its state or a closing action. “The door was closed by the guard at six” clearly describes the action. Check the meaning as well as the spelling.

Common mistakes

Incorrect

The room cleaned every day.

Correct

The room is cleaned every day.

A present simple passive needs is before the participle.

Incorrect

The letter was wrote yesterday.

Correct

The letter was written yesterday.

Use the participle written after was.

Incorrect

The doors was locked.

Correct

The doors were locked.

The plural subject doors needs were.

Incorrect

Did the parcel sent yesterday?

Correct

Was the parcel sent yesterday?

Move was before the subject in a be-passive question.

Incorrect

The work has finished by the team.

Correct

The work has been finished by the team.

A present perfect passive needs been.

Incorrect

The ticket can changed.

Correct

The ticket can be changed.

Use be after the modal in this passive.

Incorrect

The accident was happened yesterday.

Correct

The accident happened yesterday.

Happen has no object and cannot form this passive.

Incorrect

The picture was painted by she.

Correct

The picture was painted by her.

Use an object pronoun after by.

For speakers of Chinese, Japanese and Korean

Chinese doesn’t change verbs for tense in the same way as English. If you leave out be or its tense ending, practise “is made, was made, has been made” as complete groups.

Japanese and Korean have passive patterns that can describe how an event affects someone. A direct translation can give an unnatural English passive. Check whether the English verb or verb + preposition group allows a passive before moving a noun to subject position.

For any first language, test a difficult sentence in active form first. “Someone arrived the train” doesn’t work. So use “The train arrived”, without was. These checks help with possible transfer errors; they don’t describe every learner.
