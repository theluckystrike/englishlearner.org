Reported speech rules, tense changes and say vs tell

A2 to B1 About 13 minutes

The key idea

Indirect speech, one of the two main types of reported speech, tells people what someone said without quoting their exact words. Say focuses on the words. Tell focuses on the message and normally needs a person: tell someone.

What reported speech does

Cambridge Dictionary names two main types of reported speech, direct speech and indirect speech (Cambridge Dictionary, Reported speech). Direct speech gives the actual words someone said (Cambridge Dictionary, Reported speech (direct speech)). Indirect speech focuses more on the content of what someone said than on the exact words (Cambridge Dictionary, Reported speech (indirect speech)). This lesson is mostly about indirect speech.

In indirect speech, the reported clause has a different form for a statement, a question and a command (Cambridge Dictionary, Reported speech (indirect speech)):

TypeDirect speechIndirect speech
statement“I am tired.”She said (that) she was tired.
yes-no question“Are you ready?”He asked me if I was ready.
wh- question“Where do you live?”He asked me where I lived.
command“Wait here.”She told me to wait there.

A reported statement uses a that-clause. We often leave out that, especially in informal English: He said he would call me. A reported question uses if, whether or a question word. A reported command uses a to-infinitive. The sections below show each pattern.

Tense changes (backshift)

When the reporting verb is past (she said), the verb in the reported clause often moves back in time. Cambridge Dictionary calls this backshift. It explains backshift as the changes we make to the original verbs “because time has passed between the moment of speaking and the time of the report” (Cambridge Dictionary, Reported speech (indirect speech)).

Direct speechReported speech
present simple: “I work at home.”past simple: She said she worked at home.
present continuous: “I am reading.”past continuous: He said he was reading.
present perfect: “I have finished.”past perfect: She said she had finished.
present perfect continuous: “I have been waiting.”past perfect continuous: She said she had been waiting.
past simple: “I saw the film.”past perfect: He said he had seen the film.
past continuous: “I was cooking.”past perfect continuous: She said she had been cooking.
past perfect: “I had eaten.”no change: She said she had eaten.
will: “I will help.”would: She said she would help.

Present tenses move back to past tenses. The past simple and the past continuous move back to the past perfect forms. The past perfect does not change.

Modal verbs

Some modal verbs change in indirect speech and some do not (Cambridge Dictionary, Reported speech (indirect speech)). For example, can goes to could, and must for obligation usually goes to had to. Could, should, would, might and needn’t do not change:

Direct speechReported speech
shall: “I shall be late.”would (usually): She said she would be late.
shall in a question: “Shall I open it?”should: He asked if he should open it.
can: “I can swim.”could: He said he could swim.
may (possibility): “It may rain.”might: She said it might rain.
may (permission): “You may leave now.”could: She said I could leave.
must (obligation): “You must go.”had to (usually): She said I had to go.
must (speculation): “It must be late.”no change: He said it must be late.
could, should, would, might, needn’tno change
used to, ought tono change

When tenses do not change

Backshift is not always necessary. Cambridge Dictionary says “We don’t need to change the tense in indirect speech if what a person said is still true or relevant or has not happened yet.” Then both forms are correct (Cambridge Dictionary, Reported speech (indirect speech)). The tense often stays the same when:

  • the reporting verb is present and the original words are still true or relevant: She says she is tired.
  • the words are still true at the moment of reporting: He said he lives in York. (He still lives there.)
  • the event has not happened yet: She said she will call tomorrow.
Correct

She says she is tired.

Over-shifted

She says she was tired. (Her words now: “I am tired.”)

Backshift happens because time has passed between the moment of speaking and the report. Here she speaks now and the reporting verb is present, so the tense does not change.

Pronouns and words like this, here and now

Changes to pronouns depend on who reports the words (Cambridge Dictionary, Reported speech (indirect speech)). If another person reports them, I and you change. If the same person reports their own words, I does not change:

DirectReported
I (another person reports): “I am cold,” Tom said.he: Tom said he was cold.
I (the same person reports): “I will help,” I said.I (no change): I said I would help.
you (another person reports): “You are late,” Sam told Ana.she: Sam told Ana she was late.

We often change this and these and words for time and place, because the report comes later than the original words, and perhaps in a different place. These are typical changes (Cambridge Dictionary, Reported speech (indirect speech)):

DirectReported
thisthat
thesethose
nowthen
yesterdaythe day before
tomorrowthe next day / the following day
two weeks agotwo weeks before
herethere

Direct: “I saw her here two weeks ago.” Reported: He said he had seen her there two weeks before.

Reported questions

Reported yes-no questions use if or whether, and Cambridge says “If is more common than whether.” Reported wh- questions keep the question word (who, what, where and so on). In both types the reported clause is in statement form (subject + verb), not question form (Cambridge Dictionary, Reported speech (indirect speech)):

Direct

“Do you speak Spanish?”

Reported

She asked me if I spoke Spanish. / She asked me whether I spoke Spanish.

Direct

“Where do you live?”

Reported

He asked me where I lived.

Statement order (where + I + lived), not question order (where do you live).

Direct: “What time does the shop open?” Reported: She asked what time the shop opened.

Because the reported clause is in statement form, it has no do or did from the question. A reported wh- question has no question mark:

Correct

He asked me where I lived.

Incorrect

He asked me where did I live?

The reported clause needs statement word order, and the sentence ends with a full stop.

Reported commands

A reported command has a reporting clause and a reported clause that begins with a to-infinitive (Cambridge Dictionary, Reported speech (indirect speech)). With tell, the person comes before the to-infinitive: tell + indirect object + to-infinitive. Cambridge says we don’t normally use say in this way (Cambridge Dictionary, Say and tell). Other verbs that mean wanting or getting people to do something, for example advise, encourage and warn, also take a to-infinitive:

Direct: “Wait here.” Reported: She told me to wait there.

Direct: “You should rest.” Reported: He advised me to rest.

Direct: “Don’t be late.” Reported: She told me not to be late.

In a negative report, not comes before to. Cambridge shows this with warn: “The guard warned us not to enter the area.” (Cambridge Dictionary, Reported speech (indirect speech))

Say or tell

Say focuses on the words someone said and tell focuses more on the content or message of what someone said. We use say with direct speech, and we don’t normally use tell in this way (Cambridge Dictionary, Say and tell):

Correct

‘Good morning,’ she said.

Incorrect

‘Good morning,’ she told.

Tell normally takes an indirect object (the person) and a direct object (the report): She told him the train was late. Say does not take an indirect object. Instead, we use a phrase with to: Then he said to me, ‘I know you.’

CorrectWrong
He said to me that he was busy.He said me that he was busy.
He told me that he was busy.He told that he was busy.
She said she would call.She told she would call.
They told us to sit down.They said us to sit down.

We use tell without an indirect object with words such as the truth, a lie, a joke and a story: Please tell the truth.

Common mistakes

Cambridge Dictionary lists these types of error on its Reported speech (indirect speech) and Say and tell pages.

MistakeWhat happenedFix
He asked me where did I live.Question word order in a reported question.He asked me where I lived.
She said me she was ill.Say does not take an indirect object.She told me she was ill. / She said to me that she was ill.
He told that he was tired.Tell without an indirect object when we report someone’s words.He said (that) he was tired. / He told me (that) he was tired.
‘I’m sorry,’ she told.Direct speech goes with say, not tell.‘I’m sorry,’ she said.
They said us to go home.Commands are reported with tell + indirect object + to-infinitive.They told us to go home.
She asked me what I needed?A reported wh- question has no question mark.She asked me what I needed.

Reported speech in Cambridge exams

The A2 Key list of grammatical areas has “Subordinate clause following say, tell” (page 50). The B1 Preliminary list has “Simple reported speech” and “Statements, questions and commands: say, ask, tell” (page 71). Both handbooks list “reporting what people say” in their inventory of functions, notions and communicative tasks (A2 Key page 49, B1 Preliminary page 70). Sources: A2 Key handbook and B1 Preliminary handbook.

The A2 Key handbook says “A2 Key is targeted at Level A2 on the CEFR.” The B1 Preliminary handbook says “B1 Preliminary is targeted at Level B1 on the CEFR.” (both on page 3). To see what each level means, read English levels explained.

Try the quiz

Answer the questions in writing first. Then open the answer key.

  1. Rewrite with backshift: “I am reading a good book,” said Ana.
  2. Report this question. Start with She asked me: “Where do you keep the keys?”
  3. Choose said or told: He ___ me that the shop was closed.
  4. Report the command. Start with She told us: “Don’t touch the paintings.”
  5. Find the mistake: She asked me what time did the train leave.
  6. Choose is or was and say why: She says she ___ from Brazil. (Her words: “I am from Brazil.”)
Show all answers
  1. Ana said (that) she was reading a good book. Present continuous goes back to past continuous.
  2. She asked me where I kept the keys. Statement word order, no do and no question mark.
  3. told. Tell takes an indirect object (me). Say does not.
  4. She told us not to touch the paintings. In a negative report, not comes before to.
  5. Remove did and use statement word order: She asked me what time the train left.
  6. is. The reporting verb says is present and she speaks now, so there is no backshift.
