Got or gotten

B1 to B2 About 7 minutes

The key idea

The past simple of get is always got. The past participle is got in British English and got or gotten in American English.

Learners meet both got and gotten and often mix them. The choice depends on the variety of English and on the meaning. This lesson gives the three forms of get, the meanings that take gotten, the two patterns that keep got, and the same split in other verbs.

The three forms of get

Get is an irregular verb and one of the most common verbs in English. Its past simple is always got. Its past participle is got in British English and gotten in American English.

FormBritish EnglishAmerican English
Base formgetget
Past simplegotgot
Past participlegotgot or gotten

The past simple never uses gotten. "I gotten a letter" is wrong in both varieties. Gotten appears only after have, has or had.

British English uses got

In British English the past participle is got for every meaning. The perfect tenses therefore look the same as the past simple, and the auxiliary shows the difference.

  • I got an email yesterday. (past simple)
  • I have got an email from her. (present perfect, British)
  • They had got the tickets before the price rose. (past perfect, British)

American English uses gotten for a change or an acquisition

American English keeps gotten for the idea of receiving, obtaining, becoming or reaching a state. The focus is on the result of a change.

  • She has gotten a new job. (she obtained it)
  • The weather has gotten colder. (it became colder)
  • He had gotten lost before he found the station. (he reached a state)

Have got and have got to keep got

American English still uses got in two fixed patterns: possession with have got, and obligation with have got to. These patterns are not perfect tenses of the verb get. They are idioms with a present meaning.

SentenceMeaningWhere it is usual
I have got two brothers.I have two brothers.British; informal American
I have got to go.I must go.British; informal American
I have gotten two emails.I have received two emails.American
I have got two emails.I have received two emails, or I have two emails.British; the meaning comes from context

"I have got to go" is about the present. "I have gotten to go" is about an opportunity that came in the past. Do not use one for the other.

Other verbs with two participles

Get is not alone. Several verbs have a British participle in -t and an American participle in -ed. The pattern is the same: both forms are correct, and one variety prefers one form.

BaseBritish participleAmerican participle
learnlearntlearned
burnburntburned
dreamdreamtdreamed
spellspeltspelled
spoilspoiltspoiled

Both forms are understood everywhere. Choose one variety and keep it through a piece of writing.

Common mistakes

IncorrectCorrectReason
I gotten the message.I got the message.The past simple is always got.
She has got a new job in American English.She has gotten a new job.Acquisition uses gotten in American English.
He has gotten a car since 2019.He has had a car since 2019.Possession uses have, not get, with a period.
I have got to went.I have got to go."Have got to" takes the base form.
