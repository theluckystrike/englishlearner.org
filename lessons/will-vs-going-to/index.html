Will vs going to

A2 to B1

The key idea

Use be going to for a plan that you made before now and for a prediction from evidence that you can see now. Use will for a decision that you make now, an offer, a promise, a prediction or opinion, and a fact about the future.

“I will help you” or “I’m going to help you”? Both talk about the future. Often both are possible, but each form fits some situations better. Cambridge says “There is no future tense in English.” We use different forms for the future. This lesson shows when to use will, be going to, the present continuous and the present simple.

How to form each form

How to form each form
Featurewillgoing to
Formwill + base verbam / is / are + going to + base verb
PositiveI will call you.I am going to call you.
NegativeI will not (won't) call.I am not going to call.
QuestionWill you call?Are you going to call?
Short formsI’ll call. I won’t call.I’m going to call. She’s going to call.
shall, with I and we onlyWe shall call. We shan’t call. Shall we call?No shall form

Will has the same form with every subject, for example I will, she will and they will. Cambridge says “In speaking, will and shall are usually contracted to ’ll, especially after subject pronouns”, for example I’ll call you.

The present continuous is for arrangements, for example I’m meeting Tom at six. The present simple is for timetables, for example The bus leaves at 7.30. Read present simple or present continuous first.

Use going to for plans and evidence

Plans and intentions decided before now

The decision exists before you speak. You have already thought about it.

  • I'm going to study medicine. A long-term plan.
  • We're going to repaint the kitchen next weekend. Decided last week.
  • She's going to look for a new job. Her intention now.

Do not mix the future form with movement. Cambridge says “We use the present continuous form of the verb go + preposition to + noun phrase to talk about movement”. I’m going to the market. is movement. I’m going to buy some fruit. is the future form.

Predictions based on evidence you can see now

  • Look at those clouds. It's going to rain. You can see the clouds.
  • Careful! You're going to drop the plates. You can see it happening.

Use will for decisions, predictions, promises, offers and requests

Instant decisions made while speaking

  • The phone is ringing. I'll get it. Decided at the moment of speaking.
  • A: I'm cold. B: I'll close the window.

Predictions and opinions about the future

  • I think Spain will win. An opinion, no visible evidence.
  • You will love this film.
  • Prices will probably rise again.

You can use will after I think for an opinion about the future.

Promises, offers and requests

  • I'll always love you. A promise.
  • I'll carry that for you. An offer.
  • Will you help me with the dishes? A request.

The key rule

Use going to for a plan made before now. Use will for a decision made now. Cambridge says “We use will when the decision is immediate and be going to when we have already made a plan”.

Less clear

I'll visit my grandmother this weekend. I decided on Monday.

Better

I'm going to visit my grandmother this weekend. I decided on Monday.

The decision was made before now, so going to fits better.

Unnatural

I'm going to get it! (the phone is ringing, decision now)

Natural

I'll get it!

A decision made while speaking takes will.

Predictions with and without evidence

Both forms can make predictions. The difference is the evidence.

  • It's going to rain. You can see dark clouds, so the evidence is in the present.
  • I think it will rain tomorrow. An opinion. You cannot see the rain yet.

Both forms can show that you are sure. Cambridge says “Will is used when we are talking about something with absolute certainty.” It also says “We use be going to to predict something that we think is certain to happen or which we have evidence for now” and “Be going to is used when we want to emphasise our decision or the evidence in the present”.

What about shall?

In formal English, we can use shall instead of will with I and we. Cambridge says “Shall is only used for future time reference with I and we, and is more formal than will.” The Cambridge page on shall says that shall in statements is “much less common than will”.

  • We shall never forget your kindness. Formal.
  • Shall I open the window? An offer. Cambridge says “We use shall I and shall we to make offers and suggestions, and to ask for advice.”

The negative form is shan’t (shall not), for example I shan’t be long.

Common mistakes

Two of these mistakes, the present continuous for a prediction and the present simple for a decision now, are in the Cambridge list of typical errors.

Incorrect

Take a coat or you’re getting cold.

Correct

Take a coat or you’re going to get cold.

Do not use the present continuous for a prediction. Use going to or will.

Incorrect

Wait, I open the door for you.

Correct

Wait, I’ll open the door for you.

For a decision now, use ’ll. Do not use I shall or the present simple.

Incorrect

She wills to start a course.

Correct

She will start a course.

Will is a modal verb: no -s, no to after it.

Incorrect

I'll to help you.

Correct

I'll help you.

Will and going to are followed by the base verb.

Future forms in Cambridge exams

The A2 Key handbook (printed page 50) and the B1 Preliminary handbook (printed page 71) both list “Future with going to”, “Future with will and shall: offers, promises, predictions, etc.” and “Present continuous: present actions and future meaning”. B1 Preliminary (printed page 71) also lists “Was/were going to”. Both handbooks list “talking about future plans or intentions” and “making predictions” (A2 Key printed page 49, B1 Preliminary printed page 70). The handbooks say “A2 Key is targeted at Level A2 on the CEFR.” and “B1 Preliminary is targeted at Level B1 on the CEFR.”

Try the quiz

Write your answers first. Then open the answer key.

  1. I ___ (see) the dentist tomorrow. I booked it last week.
  2. The phone is ringing. I ___ (answer) it.
  3. Careful! That pile of books ___ (fall)!
  4. I think Brazil ___ (win) the next World Cup.
  5. A: Why are you buying these books? B: I ___ (study) Japanese.
  6. Don't worry, I ___ (not tell) anyone your secret.
  7. ___ I carry that bag for you? (an offer)
  8. They ___ (move) to Canada in June. The tickets are already booked.
  9. Try this cake. You ___ (love) it.
  10. Look at the sky! It ___ (snow) tonight.
Show all answers
  1. am seeing or am going to see. You booked it, so it is an arrangement and a plan.
  2. ’ll answer (will answer). A decision made while speaking.
  3. is going to fall. Evidence you can see now.
  4. will win. An opinion with no visible evidence.
  5. am going to study. An intention decided before now. Am studying is also correct if you study Japanese now.
  6. won’t tell (will not tell). A promise.
  7. Shall. Shall I ...? makes an offer.
  8. are moving or are going to move. The tickets are booked, so it is an arrangement and a plan.
  9. will love. A prediction or opinion.
  10. is going to snow. Evidence: you can see the sky.
