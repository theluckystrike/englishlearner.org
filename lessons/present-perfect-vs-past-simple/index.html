Present perfect or past simple?

"I have seen that film" or "I saw that film"? Both are correct, but they do not mean the same thing. The past simple puts an action at a finished time. The present perfect connects a past action to now.

Level: B1. Reading time: about 10 minutes.

1. How to form each tense

Past simplePresent perfect
Formverb + -ed, or an irregular past formhave / has + past participle
PositiveShe worked. They went.She has worked. They have gone.
NegativeShe didn't work.She hasn't worked.
QuestionDid she work?Has she worked?

Many common verbs are irregular: go, went, gone. See the irregular verbs list.

2. Use the past simple for a finished time

Use the past simple when the time is over, and you say when or the listener knows when.

  • I visited Rome in 2019.
  • She called me yesterday.
  • We moved here three years ago.
  • When did you arrive? A question with "when" asks for a finished time.

Time words for the past simple: yesterday, last week, last year, in 2010, two days ago, when I was a child, at 6 o'clock this morning.

3. Use the present perfect for a link to now

Experience at any time up to now

The exact time is not given and is not important.

  • I have visited Rome. At some time in my life.
  • Have you ever eaten sushi?
  • She has never been to Asia.

A period that is not finished

  • I have drunk three coffees today. Today is not over.
  • We have had a lot of rain this month.

A past action with a result now

  • I have lost my keys. I cannot find them now.
  • He has broken his leg. It is still broken.

Something that started in the past and continues now

Use "for" with a length of time and "since" with a starting point.

  • I have lived here for five years. I still live here.
  • She has worked there since 2021. She still works there.

Compare: I lived there for five years. Past simple. I do not live there now.

Just, already, yet

  • I have just finished. A very short time ago.
  • We have already eaten. Before now, perhaps sooner than expected.
  • Have you done it yet? I haven't done it yet. In questions and negatives.

American English often uses the past simple with these words: Did you eat yet? I already ate. British English prefers the present perfect.

4. The key rule

Do not use the present perfect with a finished time.

I have seen him yesterday. I saw him yesterday.

She has finished university in 2015. She finished university in 2015.

A conversation often starts in the present perfect and moves to the past simple for the details.

  • A: Have you ever been to Japan?
  • B: Yes, I have. I went there in 2022.
  • A: Did you like it?

5. Been or gone?

  • She has been to Paris. She went and came back.
  • She has gone to Paris. She is there now, or on her way.

6. Common mistakes

WrongRight
I have been there last year.I went there last year.
I live here since 2019.I have lived here since 2019.
I know her for ten years.I have known her for ten years.
When have you arrived?When did you arrive?
I have worked here since two years.I have worked here for two years.

Quiz: choose the correct form

  1. I ___ (see) that film last night.
  2. ___ you ever ___ (try) Thai food?
  3. She ___ (work) here since 2020.
  4. We ___ (buy) this house ten years ago.
  5. I can't open the door. I ___ (lose) my key.
  6. He ___ (not finish) his homework yet.
  7. When ___ they ___ (get) married?
  8. I ___ (know) Maria for a long time. We are still good friends.
  9. My grandfather ___ (live) in Madrid all his life. He died in 2015.
  10. They ___ (be) to London three times this year.
Show the answers
  1. saw. "Last night" is a finished time.
  2. Have you ever tried. Experience at any time up to now.
  3. has worked. It started in 2020 and continues now.
  4. bought. "Ago" always takes the past simple.
  5. have lost. A result now.
  6. hasn't finished. "Yet" in a negative.
  7. When did they get married? "When" asks for a finished time.
  8. have known. It continues now.
  9. lived. His life is over, so the period is finished.
  10. have been. "This year" is not finished.
