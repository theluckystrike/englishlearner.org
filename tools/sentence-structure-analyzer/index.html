About this analysis

What is a main clause?

A main clause can stand alone as a sentence. It usually has a subject and a finite verb.

Which clauses does it count?

This tool counts finite clauses, which contain a verb that can show tense. It does not count every phrase with an infinitive or an -ing form as a separate clause.

Read a word order pattern

S is the subject. V is the verb. O is an object. C is a complement that describes or identifies something. A is an adverbial, such as a place phrase.

In “She gave him a book”, the pattern is S-V-O-O. “Him” is the receiver, and “a book” is the thing given.

Four sentence types

A simple sentence has one main clause. A compound sentence has two or more main clauses.

A complex sentence has one main clause and at least one subordinate clause. A compound-complex sentence has both kinds, with at least two main clauses.

Joining nouns does not add a clause. “Tom and Mary play tennis” has one main clause.

Limits of the result

This tool uses the local compromise word library and clause rules. It can miss clauses or choose a wrong word role.

Objects and complements are possible matches. “None found” does not prove that a sentence has none. Patterns show grammatical roles, even when a question changes their order.

A full stop, a question mark or an exclamation mark does not tell the whole story. Context can change a sentence’s function.

The tool does not check grammar, grade writing or measure complexity. It does not choose between British and American usage.
