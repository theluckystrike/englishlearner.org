Article rules and limits

Choose a or an by sound

Use a before a consonant sound, as in a book or a university. Use an before a vowel sound, as in an apple or an hour.

Letters can sound different from their names in words. An MBA starts with the sound of the letter M.

Use the for a known thing

Use the when the reader knows which thing you mean. For example, I saw a cat. The cat was orange.

Singular and plural nouns

A singular countable noun usually needs a word such as a, the or my. I am a student needs a. Books are useful needs no article because it means books in general.

Some fixed phrases have no article, as in go to school. Use the school when you mean a known building.

Uncountable nouns

Do not use a or an with information or advice in their usual meanings. Use some advice or a piece of advice. Use the water in the glass for specific water.

Some nouns have countable meanings too. A coffee can mean a cup of coffee. This tool cannot check every noun meaning.

Names and places

Most names have no article, as in John or Paris. Some need the, as in the Netherlands, the United States and the Thames.

Cases that need context

The life is beautiful may refer to a life you described earlier. For life in general, write Life is beautiful.

Go to the school can be correct for a visit to a building. Go to school means attend as a student.

Herb often starts with a vowel sound in American English and an h sound in British English. Both a herb and an herb can be correct.

The checker uses common sound patterns and a small set of missing-article rules. Unusual names, abbreviations and noun meanings need your judgement.
