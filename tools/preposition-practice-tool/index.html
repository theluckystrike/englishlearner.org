The rules in short

  • at: a clock time or a point. at 3 o'clock, at noon, at the door, at the bus stop
  • on: a day, a date or a surface. on Monday, on 15 March, on the table, on the wall
  • in: a longer period or a space with limits. in May, in 1995, in the morning, in London, in the kitchen
  • by: not later than a time. by Friday, by 6 o'clock
  • with: the tool you use, or the person who is with you. with a pen, with her friends

For the full rules, the exceptions and ten more questions, read the lesson on in, on and at. For more questions on prepositions at three levels, use the grammar exercises.

A study guide to in, on, at, by and with

The ten questions above test the prepositions that learners use most often. The notes below explain the patterns behind them, list the mistakes that appear most in learner writing, and end with a written exercise that you can do without the quiz.

Time from small to large

Think of three boxes. At is for an exact point in time, on is for a single day, and in is for anything longer than a day or for a part of the day.

Prepositions of time
PrepositionUse it forExamples
atclock times, mealtimes, short holiday periods, nightat 7.30, at lunchtime, at Easter, at night
ondays of the week, dates, special dayson Tuesday, on 4 July, on my birthday, on Friday morning
inmonths, years, seasons, decades, parts of the dayin June, in 2026, in winter, in the 1990s, in the evening

Two details catch many learners. A day together with a part of the day takes on, so say on Monday morning and not in Monday morning. British English usually says at the weekend, and American English says on the weekend. Both are correct in their own variety. Use no preposition before this, next, last, every, today or tomorrow. I'll call you next Friday.

Place as a point, a surface or a space

Prepositions of place
PrepositionUse it forExamples
ata point, or a place where an activity happensat the bus stop, at the door, at work, at the cinema
ona surface, a line or a floor of a buildingon the wall, on the river, on the second floor, on page 12
ininside a space with limits, a town, a country or a regionin the box, in the kitchen, in Paris, in Brazil

Transport is a good test of the idea. You are in a car or in a taxi because you sit inside a small space. You are on a bus, on a train or on a plane because you can stand up and walk on it. For an address, use at with a house number (at 21 Park Road). With only the street name, American English uses on (on Park Road) and British English often uses in.

By, until, with, between and among

By gives a deadline. The action happens at that time or before it. Send the report by Friday. Until means that a situation continues up to that time. The shop is open until 9 p.m. By also names the doer in a passive sentence (a novel written by Orwell) and the way you travel, with no article (by bus, by train). With names a tool or a companion. Cut it with scissors. I live with my parents. Between usually points to separate, clearly named things, and among to a group seen as a whole. The café is between the bank and the post office. She felt at home among friends.

Common mistakes and corrections

Learner errors with the reason for each correction
Not correctCorrectWhy
I was born on 1998.I was born in 1998.A year is longer than a day.
See you in Monday.See you on Monday.Days take on.
She arrived to London.She arrived in London.Arrive in a city or country, arrive at a building. Do not use arrive to.
We met in the bus stop.We met at the bus stop.A bus stop is a point, not a closed space.
I go to work with bus.I go to work by bus.Use by and no article for a kind of transport.
Please finish this until Friday.Please finish this by Friday.A deadline needs by.
He is good in maths.He is good at maths.Good at is a fixed pair.

Written exercise

Write in, on, at, by or with in each gap. Then open the answer key.

  1. The meeting starts ___ 10 o'clock.
  2. My sister was born ___ March.
  3. There is a spider ___ the ceiling.
  4. Please return the library books ___ Thursday.
  5. He opened the parcel ___ a knife.
  6. We always have a party ___ New Year's Eve.
  7. I'll meet you ___ the entrance to the museum.
  8. They have lived ___ Canada since 2019.
  9. She travels to university ___ train.
  10. The keys are ___ the drawer next to the sink.
  11. I can't sleep well ___ night.
  12. The map ___ page 40 shows the old city.
Show the answers
  1. at. A clock time is a point.
  2. in. A month is longer than a day.
  3. on. A ceiling is a surface, even when the spider hangs below it.
  4. by. This is a deadline. The books can come back earlier.
  5. with. A knife is the tool.
  6. on. New Year's Eve is one special day.
  7. at. An entrance is a point.
  8. in. Use in with a country.
  9. by. Use by and no article for a kind of transport.
  10. in. A drawer is a closed space.
  11. at. The fixed phrase is at night.
  12. on. A page is a surface.
