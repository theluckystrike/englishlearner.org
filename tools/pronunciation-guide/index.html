About this guide

American dictionary forms

The local list comes from the CMU Pronouncing Dictionary. It contains 9,363 words. We convert its sound symbols to broad IPA. Read the BSD license.

Words with more than one sound

Read can be /riːd/ in the present or /rɛd/ in the past. Use can end in /s/ as a noun or /z/ as a verb. The tool shows stored forms together. It doesn't choose a meaning.

Unknown words

If a word isn't in the list, its IPA is not known here. Check a dictionary recording. Names, abbreviations and words from other languages may be missing.

Recorded voices

Every word in the list has two recordings, an American voice and a British voice. They are natural AI voices made with Deepgram. A recording says one common form of the word, so check the meaning for words like read and live. Words outside the list use a voice from your browser, if it has one.

Word stress and a listening exercise

Learners often work hard on single sounds, but stress on the wrong syllable can make a word harder to recognise than a vowel that is a little off. Turn on stress marks in the tool and look up the words in this section. The mark ˈ comes before the strongest syllable.

Stress patterns that are worth learning

  • Most two-syllable nouns and adjectives stress the first syllable. table, window, happy, yellow
  • Many two-syllable verbs stress the second syllable. begin, decide, relax, forget
  • A few words change stress when they change word class. a record stresses the first syllable, and to record the second. Present, object and permit follow the same pattern.
  • Words that end in -tion, -sion and -ic stress the syllable just before the ending. information, decision, economic
  • Compound nouns usually stress the first part. bus stop, bookshop, toothbrush

These are tendencies, not laws, so check new words with the tool or a dictionary. When you learn a new word, learn its stress together with its spelling and meaning.

Odd one out

In each group, three words share a vowel sound and one is different. Say the words aloud, then use the tool to listen to them and see their IPA. Find the odd word before you open the key.

  1. ship, sit, seat, hill
  2. cat, hat, bed, map
  3. full, pull, book, food
  4. cut, cup, put, bus
  5. said, bread, bead, head
  6. though, go, how, show
  7. word, bird, heard, beard
  8. Which word has its stress on the second syllable? happy, window, decide, table
Show the answers
  1. seat. It has the long /iː/. The others have the short /ɪ/.
  2. bed. It has /ɛ/. The others have /æ/.
  3. food. It has /uː/. Full, pull and book have /ʊ/, even though book is spelled with oo.
  4. put. It has /ʊ/. The others have /ʌ/.
  5. bead. It has /iː/. Said, bread and head have /ɛ/, so the spellings ai and ea do not always tell you the sound.
  6. how. It has /aʊ/. The others have /oʊ/ in American English and /əʊ/ in many British accents.
  7. beard. It rhymes with feared. Word, bird and heard share the vowel of her.
  8. decide. It is a verb with stress on the second syllable. The other three stress the first syllable.

English spelling often hides the sound, as answers 3, 5 and 7 show. That is why the IPA is useful. Once you can read a few symbols, a dictionary tells you the sound of any new word before you say it.
