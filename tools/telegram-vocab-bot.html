Not live yet

VocabBot is a planned Telegram bot built specifically for vocabulary study: a shared catalog of 300 English words across three levels (A2, B1, B2), each with an example sentence and a translation into Russian, Spanish, Portuguese, Indonesian, or German. It is designed to send one daily card at an hour you choose, with buttons for Know, Don't know, and a repeat of the example sentence, and to track a rolling review schedule so words you know keep coming back at longer intervals.

VocabBot is not live yet. Follow its page for launch status: tg.zovo.one/bots/vocab.

What you can use today, a daily streak bot

HabitStreakProBot

This site and the linked Telegram tools share the same developer, Michael Lip.

Tap check-ins, a running streak per habit, and a once-a-day prompt.

While VocabBot is not yet available, you can still build a daily study reminder inside Telegram. HabitStreakProBot lets you add a habit such as "review 10 words" and prompts you once a day at an hour you set, with a single tap to mark it done. It keeps a running streak per habit, so you can track when you study, paired with whatever word list or flashcard method you already use.

Seven tactics for learning vocabulary in chat

  1. Use spaced repetition, not one long list. Review a new word right after you meet it, then again the next day, then a few days later, then about two weeks later. Each successful recall pushes the word further out; a missed one resets it to day one. Use this schedule as a starting point, and repeat difficult words sooner.
  2. Cap new words per day. Three to five new words a day is plenty. Start with a batch you can review. Reduce the number if you struggle to remember the earlier words.
  3. Attach every word to one example sentence. A bare translation is easy to forget. A sentence gives the word a job to do, so you remember how it is actually used, not just what it means in isolation.
  4. Test yourself before you check the answer. Look at the word, try to recall the meaning or say a sentence with it, and only then check. Check the answer after your attempt, then revisit words you missed.
  5. Pick one fixed time and protect it. A study habit anchored to a specific hour, right after breakfast or right before bed, survives longer than one you fit in "whenever." Chat notifications work well for this because the reminder shows up where you already look.
  6. Track a streak, not a score. A visible streak can remind you to return. Use it if it helps, and restart after a missed day.
  7. Mix levels instead of finishing one before starting the next. Reviewing easier words alongside new, harder ones keeps recall sharp on both ends. Studying only brand-new words all session tends to blur them together.

Putting it together

Right now, the practical setup is to pick your own word source, whether that is a textbook list, a set of flashcards, or notes from a lesson, and use HabitStreakProBot to enforce the daily habit with a tap-to-confirm check-in and a visible streak. When VocabBot launches, it adds the missing piece: a ready-made 300-word catalog with built-in spaced repetition, so the review schedule is handled for you instead of manually tracked. Until then, follow tg.zovo.one/bots/vocab for the launch, and browse the full lineup of bots at Tiny Telegram Tools.
